SECTION 1
EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
HAVEN HOLIDAYS LIMITED
CLAIMANT
Kennedy Vaughan
DEFENDANT
Haven Holidays Limited
INTRODUCTION
This Litigation Bundle has been prepared to assist legal representatives in understanding the factual background, documentary evidence and legal issues arising from the Claimant's dispute with Haven Holidays Limited.
The purpose of this document is to present the history of the dispute in a logical, chronological and evidential format.
It is intended to enable legal representatives to identify the principal issues quickly and to reduce the time required to reconstruct events from numerous emails, correspondence, internal documentation and Subject Access Request disclosures accumulated over several years.
This document has been prepared in anticipation of formal legal proceedings should settlement not be achieved.
SUMMARY OF THE CLAIM
The Claimant alleges that Haven Holidays Limited, acting through its employees and representatives, induced him to enter into the purchase of a replacement static caravan by making representations concerning its condition and the completion of remedial works before handover.
The Claimant's evidence is that these representations were material and directly influenced the decision to proceed with the purchase.
Following completion of the transaction, the Claimant contends that those representations were not honoured.
Instead, the replacement caravan remained subject to numerous outstanding defects despite repeated assurances, repeated complaints and prolonged attempts by the Claimant to resolve matters directly with Haven.
The dispute subsequently developed over a number of years and ultimately required the Claimant to obtain extensive documentation through a Subject Access Request in order to reconstruct the chronology and understand the handling of his case.
THE CENTRAL ISSUES
The principal issues arising from this claim are:
• Representations made before the purchase of the replacement caravan.
• Promises that all identified defects would be rectified before handover.
• Reliance placed upon those representations.
• Failure to complete the promised remedial works.
• Continued occupation of a caravan that the Claimant says did not correspond with the condition represented before purchase.
• A prolonged complaint history extending over several years.
• Delay in disclosure of documentation requested through the Subject Access Request process.
• The financial, personal and practical consequences arising from the matters complained of.
CLAIMANT'S POSITION
The Claimant respectfully submits that this litigation has never been his preferred course of action.
The documentary evidence demonstrates repeated attempts to resolve the dispute directly with Haven before legal proceedings were contemplated.
The Claimant communicated extensively with Haven, pursued the internal complaints process, repeatedly requested repairs, sought disclosure of documentation and allowed numerous opportunities for matters to be resolved without Court involvement.
Only after those attempts failed did the Claimant begin preparing this litigation bundle.
The Claimant therefore respectfully submits that these proceedings represent a genuine attempt to obtain an independent determination of issues that have remained unresolved for many years.
DOCUMENTARY EVIDENCE
This bundle is supported by:
• Email correspondence.
• Internal Haven documentation.
• Subject Access Request disclosures.
• Complaint correspondence.
• Sales records.
• Snagging records.
• Photographic evidence.
• Witness evidence.
• Financial documentation.
• Chronological analysis prepared by the Claimant.
PURPOSE OF THIS BUNDLE
This document is intended to become the master litigation file upon which legal representatives can rely when advising upon liability, evidence, quantum and litigation strategy.
It is deliberately structured to distinguish between:
• established documentary evidence;
• the Claimant's witness evidence;
• legal issues arising from those facts;
• and the evidential material supporting each section.
The Claimant respectfully submits that, when considered as a whole, the evidence demonstrates a consistent chronology supporting the allegations advanced throughout this litigation bundle.

THE PARTIES
THE CLAIMANT
Name: Kennedy Vaughan
The Claimant is a private consumer who, together with his wife, purchased a static holiday caravan from Haven Holidays Limited for family use.
The purchase was made during one of the most difficult periods experienced by the Vaughan family. Their son was undergoing treatment for lymphoma, and the Claimant sought to provide a place where the family could spend valuable time together away from the pressures associated with hospital treatment and day-to-day life.
The Claimant did not purchase the caravan as an investment or for commercial gain. It was purchased to provide security, stability and enjoyment for his family during exceptionally challenging personal circumstances.
The Claimant's evidence is that those family circumstances were known to Haven representatives throughout the sales process.
The Claimant will also rely upon his own witness evidence regarding:
the negotiations leading to the purchase;
the inspection of the replacement caravan;
the assurances given before the agreement was signed;
the subsequent complaint history;
the prolonged attempts to obtain a satisfactory resolution.
THE DEFENDANT
Haven Holidays Limited
The Defendant is a national holiday park operator engaged in the sale of new and pre-owned static holiday caravans together with associated pitch agreements and ownership services.
As a professional trader dealing with consumers, the Defendant owed duties arising under:
the contractual arrangements entered into with the Claimant;
consumer protection legislation;
the Consumer Rights Act 2015;
general legal principles relating to representations made during contractual negotiations.
The Defendant acted through its employees, managers and authorised representatives throughout the events giving rise to this claim.
HAVEN REPRESENTATIVES
During the course of this dispute the Claimant dealt with a number of Haven representatives.
The principal representative involved in the sales process was Aaron, who conducted the negotiations surrounding the replacement caravan.
The Claimant's evidence is that Aaron:
conducted the sales discussions;
inspected the replacement caravan with the Claimant;
discussed the identified defects;

Other Haven employees subsequently became involved through customer services, complaints handling and the Subject Access Request process.
Their involvement is addressed within later sections of this bundle together with the supporting documentary evidence.
THE NATURE OF THE DISPUTE
The Defendant disputes liability.
The Claimant respectfully maintains that Haven Holidays Limited failed to honour material representations made before the purchase of the replacement caravan and failed to resolve the resulting dispute despite numerous opportunities over several years.
The purpose of this litigation is to enable the Court to determine the factual issues arising between the parties and, if liability is established, to determine the appropriate remedy.

representations were made during negotiations;
those representations were relied upon;

SECTION 6
THE UPGRADE TO THE SECOND CARAVAN
Introduction
The Claimant respectfully submits that the decision to upgrade from the original caravan to the replacement caravan forms the central issue in this litigation.
The Claimant's case is not that he independently sought to replace a satisfactory caravan without reason.
Rather, the evidence will demonstrate that the upgrade arose following discussions and representations made by Haven's sales representatives, who actively promoted the benefits of purchasing a newer caravan.
The Claimant entered into those discussions believing that Haven would continue to act honestly and in his family's best interests.
The Existing Relationship
By the time discussions concerning an upgrade commenced, the Claimant had already established a relationship with Haven.
Having previously purchased a caravan through Haven, the Claimant regarded himself as an existing customer who had every reason to trust the advice and recommendations provided by Haven's representatives.
The Claimant had no reason to believe that the representations subsequently made during the upgrade negotiations would prove unreliable.
Sales Discussions
During discussions concerning the replacement caravan, Haven's representative presented the proposed upgrade as a significant improvement over the existing caravan.
The Claimant's evidence is that the replacement caravan was described as offering a better ownership experience and was represented as an appropriate choice for the Claimant and his family.
These discussions were conducted in circumstances where the Claimant reasonably believed that Haven's representative was providing accurate information upon which he could safely rely.
The Inspection
Before agreeing to proceed, the Claimant and Mrs Vaughan carried out a detailed inspection of the replacement caravan.
Rather than accepting the caravan without question, they carefully examined its condition and identified numerous defects requiring attention.
The Claimant's evidence is that these defects were discussed openly with Haven's sales representative before any agreement was signed.
The Claimant made it clear that he was unwilling to purchase the caravan in its existing condition.
Assurances Given
The Claimant's evidence is that Haven's representative responded by providing repeated assurances that every defect identified during the inspection would be rectified before the caravan was handed over.
These assurances were clear and, in the Claimant's recollection, left no doubt that the remedial works would be completed before completion of the transaction.
The Claimant will say that these assurances were not casual remarks or expressions of future intention.
They were presented as firm commitments and formed a fundamental part of the negotiations leading to the agreement.
Pressure to Proceed
The Claimant also recalls being informed that there was considerable interest in the replacement caravan from other prospective purchasers.
The effect of these statements was to create a sense of urgency.
The Claimant believed that delaying his decision could result in the caravan being sold to another customer.
The Claimant respectfully submits that this commercial pressure formed part of the circumstances in which the decision to proceed was made.
Whether other purchasers genuinely existed is a matter that the Defendant may address.
However, the Claimant's evidence is that the statements influenced his decision-making and reduced the opportunity for further consideration.
Reliance Upon Haven
Throughout these negotiations, the Claimant relied upon the expertise and integrity of Haven's representative.
He believed:
the identified defects would be fully rectified;
the caravan would be handed over in the promised condition;
Haven would honour the commitments made during the negotiations.
Had those assurances not been given, the Claimant's evidence is that he would not have entered into the agreement on the terms proposed.
Significance
The Claimant respectfully submits that the negotiations surrounding the upgrade are central to the determination of this claim.
This is because they establish:
the representations made before the agreement;
the condition of the caravan at the point of inspection;
the promises allegedly made by Haven;
the Claimant's reliance upon those promises;
the basis upon which the Claimant ultimately decided to proceed.
These matters underpin the allegations of misrepresentation, breach of contract and failure to deliver the caravan in the condition represented before purchase.
Conclusion
The Claimant respectfully submits that the upgrade transaction was entered into honestly and in good faith.
He relied upon Haven's expertise, experience and assurances.
The evidence will show that those assurances became the foundation upon which the agreement was reached.
The dispute that followed arose not because the Claimant changed his expectations, but because, in his case, the commitments made before the agreement were not fulfilled after the agreement had been concluded.

Introduction
The Claimant respectfully submits that this case extends beyond a dispute concerning caravan defects.
The central issue is whether representations made by Haven Holidays Limited, through its sales representative, induced the Claimant to enter into a transaction that he would otherwise not have entered into on the same terms.
The Claimant's evidence is that those representations were relied upon and that the subsequent failure to honour them resulted in significant financial loss, inconvenience and prolonged distress.
The Relationship of Trust
The Claimant did not enter the transaction as a result of a single sales conversation.
Over a period of time, a relationship of trust developed between the Claimant, his wife and Haven's sales representative.
The Claimant's evidence is that personal matters were discussed openly during this period.
The Haven representative became aware of the Claimant's family circumstances, including the serious illness of the Claimant's son, which had been one of the principal reasons for purchasing a holiday caravan.
The representative also discussed aspects of his own personal circumstances.
These conversations contributed to a relationship in which the Claimant believed he could place trust in the assurances being given.
The Claimant respectfully submits that this background is relevant when considering why the representations made during the sales process carried significant weight.
Reliance
The Claimant's decision to proceed with the purchase was based upon several important assumptions created by Haven's representations.
These included the understanding that:
every identified defect would be rectified before handover;
the caravan would be delivered in the promised condition;
Haven would honour the commitments made during negotiations;
any remaining issues would be addressed promptly and professionally.
The Claimant's evidence is that these were not merely expectations but assurances given during negotiations which directly influenced the decision to proceed.
Causation
The Claimant respectfully submits that there is a direct connection between the representations made before purchase and the losses subsequently suffered.
Had those representations not been made, or had the Claimant been informed that the identified defects would remain unresolved, the Claimant would not have proceeded with the transaction in its existing form.

SECTION 17
The Subject Access Request (SAR) and Documentary Disclosure
17.1 Purpose of the Subject Access Request
As the dispute continued and numerous issues remained unresolved, the Claimant became increasingly concerned about how Haven Holidays had internally recorded his complaints and managed his case.
In order to obtain a complete understanding of the internal communications, records and decisions relating to his caravan purchase and subsequent complaints, the Claimant submitted a formal Subject Access Request ("SAR") under the provisions of the UK General Data Protection Regulation and the Data Protection Act 2018.
The request sought disclosure of all personal data held by Haven relating to the Claimant, including internal correspondence, complaint records, inspection reports, notes, emails and other documentation relevant to the dispute.
The request was made not only to understand how Haven had handled the matter but also to ensure complete transparency.
17.2 Statutory Duty
Upon receiving a valid Subject Access Request, Haven became subject to statutory obligations requiring it to provide the requested personal data within the prescribed legal timescale unless a lawful exemption applied.
The purpose of these statutory obligations is to ensure openness and transparency between organisations and individuals whose personal information they hold.
Compliance with these obligations is fundamental to maintaining confidence in the complaint process.
17.3 Delay in Disclosure
Despite these legal obligations, the requested documentation was not provided within the expected timeframe.
Instead, the Claimant experienced a prolonged period of delay before disclosure was eventually made.
During that period, the Claimant remained unable to understand the full extent of Haven's internal handling of his complaints or assess the accuracy of the representations that had been made throughout the dispute.
The delay significantly prolonged an already lengthy dispute.
17.4 Effect of the Delay
The delay had practical consequences.
Without access to the requested documentation, the Claimant was unable to:
fully evaluate Haven's internal decision-making;
identify inconsistencies within the complaint handling process;
properly assess the evidence available to support his claim;
prepare legal proceedings on a fully informed basis.
The Claimant therefore remained at a considerable disadvantage for many months.
17.5 Disclosure Raises Further Questions
When disclosure was eventually received, the documents raised additional questions concerning Haven's handling of the matter.
Rather than bringing clarity, aspects of the disclosure appeared inconsistent with earlier explanations previously provided to the Claimant.
The documentary evidence will be relied upon separately within this bundle where those inconsistencies arise.
17.6 Transparency
The Claimant respectfully submits that transparency should have been evident from the outset.
Instead, obtaining documentation required persistent requests over an extended period.
The unnecessary delay increased the Claimant's frustration, prolonged the dispute and contributed to a continuing loss of confidence in Haven's willingness to resolve matters fairly.
17.7 Evidential Importance
The Subject Access Request is significant not simply because disclosure was delayed.
Its importance lies in the fact that it provides an independent documentary record against which Haven's previous explanations and representations can be assessed.
Where differences exist between documentary records and statements made to the Claimant, those inconsistencies will be highlighted elsewhere within this litigation bundle.
Legal Commentary
The Court is invited to consider the chronology of the Subject Access Request alongside the wider pattern of conduct demonstrated throughout this claim.
Whilst the delay in itself is not the primary cause of action, it forms part of the broader factual background illustrating repeated delays, poor communication and a lack of timely resolution that characterised Haven's handling of the Claimant's concerns.
