WHY THE CLAIM HAS BEEN BROUGHT BEFORE THE COURT
VAUGHAN v HAVEN HOLIDAYS LTD
Introduction
This document explains why the Claimant has reached the stage of preparing for legal proceedings. It is intended to provide the Court with a clear overview of the background to the dispute and the steps taken to resolve the matter before litigation was considered.
Background
The Claimant purchased a holiday caravan from Haven Holidays Ltd with the intention of providing a safe and enjoyable environment for family use. During ownership, discussions took place concerning an upgrade to a replacement caravan. The Claimant contends that representations made during the sales process materially influenced those decisions.
The Dispute
Following delivery of the replacement caravan, the Claimant identified numerous defects and raised concerns with Haven Holidays Ltd. Complaints were made over an extended period and the Claimant maintains that many issues remained unresolved despite repeated requests for investigation and remedial action.
Attempts to Resolve the Matter
Before considering litigation, the Claimant pursued direct communication with Haven Holidays Ltd, submitted formal complaints, retained copies of correspondence, and exercised the right to request personal information through a Subject Access Request. These steps were taken in an effort to understand the issues fully and to resolve the dispute without the need for court proceedings.
Preparation of the Trial Bundle
The Trial Bundle has been assembled to present the evidence in a structured and transparent manner. It includes witness statements, a master chronology, documentary evidence, correspondence, Subject Access Request material, financial loss schedules, legal research and trial preparation documents. Each section is cross-referenced so that the Court can trace factual assertions to the supporting evidence.
Why Court Proceedings May Be Necessary
The Claimant's position is that reasonable efforts have been made to resolve the dispute without litigation. Should those efforts ultimately prove unsuccessful, the Claimant respectfully seeks the Court's determination of the issues in dispute based upon the evidence presented and the applicable law.
Purpose of this Document
This document should be read alongside the Executive Summary, Witness Statements, Master Chronology, Document Evidence, Correspondence, Subject Access Request, Financial Loss and Legal Research & Authorities sections of the Trial Bundle. It provides context for the claim and explains why the bundle has been prepared.

1. Introduction
I make this statement from my own knowledge unless otherwise indicated. The purpose of this statement is to explain, in chronological order, how my dispute with Haven developed and why I believe I have suffered loss.
2. Family Circumstances
When the caravan was purchased my family was living through an exceptionally difficult period because my son had been diagnosed with lymphoma and had begun treatment. My wife and I wanted somewhere peaceful where our family could spend time together away from hospitals and the stress of treatment. This background is important because it explains why the purchase meant so much to us.
3. Decision to Purchase
I chose Haven because I believed I was dealing with a well-established national company. I expected that representations made by its staff could be trusted and that any issues would be dealt with professionally.
4. Upgrade Process
Following discussions with Haven representatives, I was encouraged to upgrade to another caravan. Before agreeing to proceed, I sought reassurance that the replacement caravan would be completed to the promised standard before occupation.
5. Inspection Before Occupation
My wife and I inspected the replacement caravan with Aaron. During that inspection we identified numerous defects and snagging items. We did not ignore those issues. Instead, we raised them before taking possession. Aaron assured us that the outstanding items would be completed before we moved in. Those assurances were a significant factor in our decision to continue.
6. Occupation
When we returned, our original caravan had already been removed and our belongings transferred. We therefore found ourselves committed to the replacement caravan despite the outstanding defects. In my view, the caravan was not handed over in the condition that had been promised.
