# The Quaerens Product Design Principles™

Version: 1.0  
Status: Approved Governing Product Standard

## Purpose

The Quaerens Product Design Principles™ govern how every Quaerens product, page, workflow and interface should be designed.

They apply to:

- Public website pages
- Consumer Complaint Centres™
- Complaint Packs™
- QCMS™
- QCMS Operations™
- Operations Centre™
- Case Workspace™
- Client Portal™
- Partner Portal™
- Operations CRM™
- Emails
- Reports
- Documents
- Internal tools
- Future intelligence capabilities

These principles protect clarity, consistency, usability and long-term quality as The Quaerens Platform™ grows.

The Experience Principles define how people should feel.

The Product Design Principles define how Quaerens products should work.

## The Design Purpose

Every Quaerens product should reduce uncertainty and help someone move forward with greater understanding and confidence.

Design is not decoration.

Design is how the platform:

- Explains
- Guides
- Organises
- Reassures
- Supports decisions
- Makes the next step clear

Good product design within Quaerens is measured by whether a person understands their position more clearly, knows what evidence matters, sees the next action and can make an informed choice.

## Principle 1: Evidence First

Evidence should shape the product experience.

The platform should help people understand:

- What happened
- What evidence exists
- What evidence is missing
- Why the evidence matters
- What should happen next

Do not encourage argument before facts are organised.

Evidence should come before pressure, escalation or complaint drafting wherever possible.

## Principle 2: One Truth, One Place

Important information should have one authoritative source.

Avoid:

- Duplicate case records
- Conflicting status labels
- Repeated data entry
- Multiple versions of the same truth
- Unclear ownership of information

Every important fact should have one trusted home.

When the platform displays the same information in different places, those displays should reflect the same source rather than becoming independent records.

## Principle 3: Every Screen Has One Primary Purpose

Every page, panel, workflow and screen should answer one main question.

Examples:

- Operations Centre™: What should I work on next?
- Case Workspace™: What is happening in this complaint and what should I do next?
- Complaint Pack™: What information is needed to prepare a clear complaint?

Avoid screens that try to solve too many unrelated problems.

If the purpose of a screen cannot be explained in one sentence, the design is not ready.

## Principle 4: Every Interaction Moves Forward

Every meaningful action should create progress.

Avoid:

- Dead ends
- Unnecessary confirmation screens
- Repeated questions
- Unclear buttons
- Actions that do not update visible state
- Navigation without purpose

Every click should help someone understand, decide, organise or progress.

Progress may be small. It may be saving a draft, identifying missing evidence, clarifying a status or showing the next route. But the user should never wonder whether their action mattered.

## Principle 5: Show the Next Step

Every product experience should make the next logical action visible.

Users should not need to interpret the system to understand what to do next.

Where appropriate, show:

- Recommended next action
- Who is responsible
- What is missing
- When action is due
- What will happen after completion

The platform should guide without taking away human judgement.

## Principle 6: Guide Before Complexity

Introduce complexity gradually.

Begin with what someone needs to know now.

Reveal secondary detail when it becomes relevant.

Use:

- Clear summaries
- Progressive disclosure
- Accessible explanations
- Logical grouping
- Plain-language labels

Do not expose internal complexity merely because the platform contains it.

A powerful system should feel structured, not crowded.

## Principle 7: Complete Before Starting

Help people finish meaningful work before beginning more work.

Products should support:

- Completion
- Focus
- Momentum
- Clear priorities
- Reduced context switching

Examples include:

- Ready To Complete Today
- Start Working
- Today’s Mission™
- Complaint Readiness™

The platform should reward progress, not activity for its own sake.

## Principle 8: Professional by Default

Every output and interaction should feel professionally prepared.

This includes:

- Clear structure
- Consistent language
- Appropriate spacing
- Calm visual hierarchy
- Accurate labels
- Reliable behaviour
- Well-organised documents
- Clear evidence presentation

Professional should not mean cold, legalistic or intimidating.

It should mean:

Calm.  
Reliable.  
Organised.  
Trustworthy.

## Principle 9: Consistency Creates Trust

Use established patterns consistently.

This includes:

- Terminology
- Buttons
- Statuses
- Navigation
- Colours
- Icons
- Document structures
- Complaint stages
- Confirmation messages
- Error handling

Do not invent new labels for established concepts.

Use approved terminology such as:

- The Quaerens Platform™
- Complaint Packs™
- QCMS™
- QCMS Operations™
- Operations Centre™
- Case Workspace™
- Complaint Journey™
- Complaint Readiness™
- Platform Users
- Complaint Managers

Consistency helps people understand where they are, what they are doing and what happens next.

## Principle 10: Mobile Is Not Secondary

Every public and operational experience should work clearly on mobile.

Mobile design must not be treated as a reduced desktop version.

Design for:

- Touch
- Small screens
- Clear reading order
- Document upload
- Form completion
- Clear actions
- No horizontal overflow
- Appropriate spacing
- Accessible controls

Important guidance must not disappear on smaller screens.

If a person cannot complete a core journey on mobile, the product is not complete.

## Principle 11: Accessibility Is Part of Quality

Accessibility is not an optional enhancement.

Design should support:

- Keyboard navigation
- Screen readers
- Visible focus states
- Sufficient colour contrast
- Clear labels
- Logical headings
- Reduced motion
- Understandable error messages
- Large enough touch targets
- Information that is not communicated by colour alone

An experience that excludes people is not complete.

## Principle 12: Technology Supports People

Technology should:

- Organise
- Recommend
- Explain
- Highlight
- Reduce repetition
- Identify missing information
- Preserve consistency
- Support decisions

Technology should not:

- Pretend to replace professional judgement
- Hide uncertainty
- Make unsupported conclusions
- Pressure people
- Create false confidence
- Remove accountability

People remain responsible for judgement and important decisions.

## Principle 13: Clear States, Clear Responsibility

Every complaint, task, document or workflow item should make its state clear.

Where relevant, show:

- Current stage
- Responsible person
- Waiting reason
- Due date
- Readiness
- Blockers
- Next action
- Expected milestone

Avoid vague states such as:

- In progress
- Pending
- Open

unless the meaning is clearly explained.

Clear state reduces uncertainty for users, clients and staff.

## Principle 14: Design for Momentum

Every complaint should have visible momentum.

The platform should help people understand:

- What has been completed
- What is currently happening
- What is blocking progress
- What happens next
- What can be completed today

No complaint should appear to disappear into an unclear process.

Momentum does not mean rushing. It means the route remains visible.

## Principle 15: Separate Responsibilities

Each platform system must have one clear responsibility.

Maintain the established boundaries between:

- Complaint Packs™
- QCMS™
- QCMS Operations™
- Operations CRM™
- Client Portal™
- Partner Portal™

Do not combine systems simply because integration is technically possible.

Integration should connect responsibilities.

It should not erase them.

## Principle 16: Build Once, Improve Forever

Prefer reusable patterns over one-off solutions.

Build shared:

- Components
- Language patterns
- Evidence structures
- Journey stages
- Validation models
- Document structures
- Accessibility behaviours
- Status models

Future improvement should strengthen the shared platform rather than create fragmented alternatives.

When a new page, builder or operational module needs a pattern that already exists, improve the shared pattern rather than creating a disconnected version.

## Principle 17: Design for Ten Years

Prefer durable design decisions.

Avoid:

- Short-lived visual trends
- Excessive animation
- Novelty interactions
- Unnecessary dependencies
- Fragile architecture
- Product decisions driven only by current fashion

The platform should feel modern without becoming dated quickly.

Durable design protects future users from constant reinvention.

## Principle 18: Measure Value, Not Noise

Product success should be measured through useful outcomes.

Examples:

- Complaint Packs completed
- Evidence gathered
- Complaints moved forward
- Complaints completed
- Missing information resolved
- Time saved
- User confidence
- Clear next actions
- Reduced delays

Do not focus only on:

- Page views
- Click volume
- Screen time
- Feature usage without purpose

The strongest product metric is whether the platform helps people make progress.

## Principle 19: Fail Safely and Clearly

When something goes wrong:

- Explain what happened
- Preserve work where possible
- Explain what was not completed
- Show the next recovery step
- Do not imply success when an action failed
- Do not blame the user
- Do not hide errors

Failure states should reduce uncertainty, not increase it.

A failed action should still leave the user with a clear path forward.

## Principle 20: Every Product Must Earn Its Place

Before building a feature, ask:

- Does it reduce work?
- Does it improve clarity?
- Does it strengthen evidence?
- Does it increase confidence?
- Does it support informed choice?
- Does it help someone move forward?
- Does it strengthen the wider platform?

If not:

Redesign it.

If it cannot satisfy those questions:

Do not build it.

## The Product Design Test

Before approving any product, page, screen or workflow, ask:

1. Is its primary purpose immediately clear?
2. Does it reduce uncertainty?
3. Does it show the next step?
4. Does it reduce unnecessary work?
5. Does it preserve one trusted source of truth?
6. Does it work clearly on mobile?
7. Is it accessible?
8. Does it use approved terminology?
9. Does it support people rather than replace judgement?
10. Does it strengthen the connected platform?

If not:

The design is not ready.

## The Quaerens Product Standard

Every Quaerens product should feel:

- Clear
- Calm
- Focused
- Reliable
- Connected
- Professional
- Human
- Evidence-led
- Actionable
- Easy to understand

Never:

- Cluttered
- Fragmented
- Ambiguous
- Overwhelming
- Decorative without purpose
- Technically impressive but unhelpful
- Inconsistent
- Pressure-driven

The standard is not perfection for its own sake. It is the practical discipline required to help people make progress with less uncertainty.

## Closing Declaration

The Quaerens Platform™ should never make people work harder to understand their complaint.

It should organise complexity.

Reveal what matters.

Show the next step.

Support informed decisions.

And help people move forward with greater confidence.

We don't build features because we can.

We build them because they help people move forward.

Evidence.  
Clarity.  
Results.
