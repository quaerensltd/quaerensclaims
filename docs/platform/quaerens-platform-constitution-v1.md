# The Quaerens Platform Constitution™ v1.0

Status: Highest permanent governing standard
Authority: The Quaerens Platform™
Scope: Every framework, product, workspace, partner and architectural decision

## 1. Constitutional authority

This Constitution is the highest governing document of The Quaerens Platform.

Every framework, product, CRM workspace, implementation, future developer, operator, supplier, partner and architectural decision must follow it.

Where another platform document, framework standard, category specification, implementation note or established practice conflicts with this Constitution, this Constitution takes precedence. Subordinate documents must be interpreted consistently with it and corrected through an explicit governance change when a conflict is discovered.

This Constitution defines responsibility and architectural authority. It does not by itself authorise implementation, release, commercial commitment or legal representation.

## 2. Mission

Quaerens exists to help consumers understand, organise and present complex disputes before deciding what action to take.

Consumer problems are often made harder by fragmented evidence, unclear chronology, uncertain financial impact, unfamiliar processes and premature escalation. Quaerens reduces that uncertainty by transforming scattered information into structured, evidence-linked case material and clear next steps.

Its mission is to:

- make professional-quality case preparation more accessible;
- help consumers understand what information matters;
- identify evidence that is available, missing or not applicable;
- organise facts before conclusions are drawn;
- clarify possible routes without promising outcomes;
- support informed decisions before escalation, expenditure or commitment;
- enable professional and operational teams to work from prepared, auditable case information.

Quaerens is evidence-first. Evidence is organised before opinion is offered, limitations are made visible, and recommendations must remain proportionate to the available information.

## 3. What The Quaerens Platform is

Quaerens is not, by architectural identity:

- a claims company;
- a law firm;
- a lead-generation website.

Quaerens is an evidence-first consumer support platform.

The platform helps consumers prepare, understand and progress disputes through independent but connected frameworks. Particular services or authorised partners may operate within relevant professional, regulatory or commercial boundaries, but those arrangements do not change the platform's core architectural identity.

The platform does not treat consumers as leads. It prepares cases, receives intentional requests for support and enables controlled operational delivery.

## 4. The four-framework model

The Quaerens Platform consists of four independent but connected frameworks. Each framework has one primary responsibility, a defined owner, controlled inputs and distinct outputs.

### 4.1 Framework A — Complaint Pack Framework™

#### Purpose

To enable consumers to prepare professional, evidence-first complaint packs through a free, browser-first and consumer-controlled experience.

#### Ownership

Framework A owns the DIY complaint-preparation journey and the shared architecture used by every configured Complaint Pack Builder.

#### Responsibilities

- guide a consumer through structured case preparation;
- collect and organise information within the browser-first privacy model;
- structure chronology, evidence and financial impact;
- identify evidence readiness and missing evidence;
- generate professional complaint materials;
- preserve consumer control over whether and how material is submitted;
- offer Guided Support only as a deliberate choice after preparation.

#### Outputs

- professional Complaint Pack;
- Executive Summary;
- Timeline;
- Evidence Schedule;
- Financial Schedule;
- Complaint Letter;
- Cover Email;
- Submission Checklist;
- Response Tracker;
- Complaint Pack Quality indicator.

Framework A does not qualify support requests, determine pricing, route cases, assign CRM workspaces or conduct operational service delivery.

### 4.2 Framework B — Professional Case Builder Framework™

#### Purpose

To provide one internal, evidence-first professional system for guided support, assessments and managed consumer dispute services.

#### Ownership

Framework B owns professional case-building, assessment structure and the creation of professional evidence documents across configured service routes.

#### Responsibilities

- support professional client intake;
- collect, review and organise evidence;
- build an evidence-linked chronology;
- record financial impact and exposure;
- track missing evidence;
- support professional findings, recommendations and internal notes;
- maintain appropriate audit and document controls;
- produce consistent professional documentation;
- integrate outputs with authorised operational workflows.

#### Outputs

- Professional Assessment Reports;
- Evidence Files;
- Client Summaries;
- Professional Case Files;
- Complaint Preparation Files;
- Referral Packs where required.

Framework B does not replace CRM, own operational pipelines, qualify public Guided Support Requests or reproduce the consumer DIY journey.

### 4.3 Framework C — Quaerens Intake Gateway™

#### Purpose

To provide the central intake, qualification and routing layer between the public platform and operational CRM workspaces.

#### Ownership

Framework C owns Guided Support intake and every decision required before a Prepared Case becomes an operational case.

#### Responsibilities

- receive intentional Guided Support Requests;
- receive Prepared Cases rather than generic leads;
- validate the intake contract;
- qualify requests;
- determine the applicable product and pricing journey;
- apply configurable business rules;
- route and assign Prepared Cases;
- balance approved workspace and partner capacity;
- control when an operational CRM case is created;
- maintain intake status and assignment history.

#### Outputs

- qualified or rejected intake decision;
- selected product and pricing journey;
- assignment decision;
- routing decision;
- controlled operational-case creation instruction;
- Gateway reference and auditable intake record;
- prepared handoff to the assigned Operations CRM workspace.

Framework C does not prepare consumer Complaint Packs, perform professional assessments or undertake operational CRM work.

### 4.4 Framework D — Operations CRM™

#### Purpose

To deliver authorised operational services through one shared CRM interface with multiple controlled workspaces.

#### Ownership

Framework D owns operational delivery after a case has been validly created and assigned.

#### Responsibilities

- manage people and authorised users;
- manage tasks and activity;
- manage communications;
- manage operational pipeline and workflow;
- record status, ownership and progress;
- support role-based work within workspace boundaries;
- associate professional documents and outputs with operational cases;
- preserve workspace data isolation and permissions.

#### Outputs

- completed operational tasks;
- communication records;
- pipeline and case status;
- callbacks, appointments and assigned activity;
- service-delivery records;
- operational reporting and audit history;
- controlled case outcomes and closure records.

Framework D does not accept website enquiries directly, decide public routing, recreate Complaint Pack Builders or replace the Professional Case Builder.

## 5. The golden rule

Every framework has one responsibility.

No framework performs another framework's job.

Integration connects responsibilities; it does not merge them. Convenience, speed or category-specific pressure does not justify bypassing a framework boundary.

When ownership is unclear, work must pause at the architectural decision point until the responsible framework is identified.

## 6. Public website responsibility

The public website educates.

The public website prepares.

The public website never creates CRM records.

It may present public information, configured Complaint Pack Builders and clear choices. Where a consumer deliberately requests Guided Support, the public platform may transmit a controlled Guided Support Request to the Intake Gateway. It must not decide which CRM or partner receives the case and must not embed operational routing logic.

## 8. End-to-end responsibility flow

The governed platform flow is:

1. The public website educates the consumer.
2. Framework A prepares a consumer-controlled Complaint Pack.
3. The consumer chooses either self-submission or Guided Support.
4. A Guided Support Request enters Framework C.
5. Framework C qualifies, prices, routes and assigns the Prepared Case.
6. Only after valid assignment is an operational case created in Framework D.
7. Framework D manages people, tasks, communication, pipeline and status.
8. Where professional assessment or managed case documentation is required, Framework B produces the professional evidence documents used by the operational workflow.

Information may move between frameworks through controlled contracts. Responsibility does not move with it.

## 9. Architectural principles

### 9.1 One CRM

There is one shared Operations CRM architecture. New operational workspaces reuse it rather than receiving duplicated CRM interfaces.

### 9.2 Multiple workspaces

Workspace determines authorised data, users, permissions, routing availability and other approved operational boundaries. It does not create a different CRM product.

### 9.3 One Complaint Pack Framework

Every public Complaint Pack Builder is a configured implementation of one shared Framework A architecture.

### 9.4 One Professional Case Builder Framework

Every managed assessment and professional case-building route is configured through one shared Framework B architecture.

### 9.5 One Intake Gateway

Every Guided Support Request enters one controlled Framework C intake and routing model.

### 9.6 One Operations Platform

The four frameworks form one coherent platform. Shared governance, controlled contracts and clear ownership prevent fragmentation while preserving framework independence.

## 10. Platform philosophy

### Evidence before opinion

Material facts, source documents and limitations must be organised before conclusions are presented.

### Organisation before escalation

A consumer should understand and structure the case before committing to an escalation route.

### Preparation before action

The platform should improve readiness before a complaint, referral, assessment or operational intervention begins.

### Clarity before complexity

Interfaces, documents and decisions should make the case easier to understand. Complexity must be introduced only when the matter requires it.

### Frameworks before features

New capability belongs within the framework that owns it. A feature must not become a standalone architecture merely because it is new.

### Consistency before expansion

Growth must extend proven systems, vocabulary, controls and quality standards before adding categories, workspaces or partners.

## 11. Development and architectural decision rules

Before beginning any work:

1. identify the consumer, professional, intake or operational problem;
2. determine which framework owns the responsibility;
3. read the Constitution and the owning framework's current standard;
4. inspect existing shared capabilities before proposing new ones;
5. configure or extend the owning framework;
6. identify every affected configured route or workspace;
7. define regression, security and boundary tests;
8. document any approved shared architectural change.

Every contributor must follow these prohibitions:

- never create duplicate architecture;
- never duplicate workflows;
- never duplicate document engines;
- never duplicate CRM interfaces;
- never embed CRM creation or routing logic in public pages;
- never create category-specific systems where configuration is sufficient;
- never bypass the Intake Gateway for Guided Support;
- never fragment the platform.

If requested work conflicts with these rules, the conflict must be surfaced before implementation. Existing duplication is technical and governance debt; it is not precedent for further duplication.

## 12. Framework contracts

Frameworks must remain independently governable and loosely coupled. Connections between them require explicit contracts defining:

- purpose and lawful or authorised basis;
- data supplied and data minimisation;
- owning framework before and after the handoff;
- identity, workspace and access controls;
- status and error handling;
- audit requirements;
- version compatibility;
- retention and deletion responsibility;
- failure behaviour that does not bypass governance.

A contract may transfer information or initiate authorised work. It must not silently transfer architectural ownership.

## 13. Consumer and evidence safeguards

- Consumer information must be collected for a defined platform purpose.
- Browser-first DIY information remains consumer-controlled until deliberately submitted.
- Guided Support must be an intentional choice.
- The platform must distinguish evidence, professional analysis and operational status.
- Missing information and limitations must be visible.
- Scores, assessments and recommendations must not be represented as guaranteed outcomes.
- Public journeys must not conceal lead-generation or direct-to-CRM behaviour.
- Access to professional and operational material must follow authorised roles and workspace boundaries.
- Framework outputs must preserve traceability appropriate to their purpose.

## 14. Products, services and partners

A product, service category, workspace or partner is not a fifth framework.

New products and partners must use the existing four-framework model:

- public preparation through Framework A where applicable;
- professional case-building through Framework B where applicable;
- Guided Support qualification and routing through Framework C;
- operational delivery through Framework D.

Commercial models, category terminology and partner arrangements may be configured within the owning framework. They must not redefine platform architecture or weaken responsibility boundaries.

## 15. Governance hierarchy

Platform governance follows this order of authority:

1. **The Quaerens Platform Constitution™** — highest governing authority.
2. **Framework architecture standards** — permanent responsibility and design rules for Frameworks A, B, C and D.
3. **Shared technical and operational standards** — contracts, security, accessibility, quality and release controls.
4. **Configured category and workspace specifications** — permitted variations within a framework.
5. **Implementation documentation** — current routes, components, functions and deployment procedures.

A lower-level document cannot override a higher-level document. Conflict must be resolved by correcting the lower-level rule or by an explicitly approved constitutional amendment.

## 16. Versioning and constitutional change

Each framework evolves independently under its own documented versioning rules. A framework change must preserve this Constitution and must not appropriate another framework's responsibility.

This document establishes:

**The Quaerens Platform Constitution™ v1.0**

The Constitution evolves only by explicit approval. A constitutional amendment must:

1. identify the principle or responsibility being changed;
2. state why framework-level change is insufficient;
3. describe the effect on all four frameworks;
4. identify affected governance documents and implementations;
5. receive explicit platform-authority approval;
6. create a new documented constitutional version;
7. preserve an auditable record of the superseded version.

Silence, convenience, implementation drift or category precedent cannot amend the Constitution.

## 17. Constitutional acceptance test

A platform decision conforms to this Constitution only when:

- its owning framework is identified;
- it extends or configures that framework rather than duplicating it;
- it respects the single-responsibility boundary of every other framework;
- public pages do not create CRM records or decide routing;
- Guided Support passes through the Intake Gateway;
- operational delivery remains within the shared CRM architecture;
- professional case documents remain within the Professional Case Builder architecture;
- DIY Complaint Packs remain within the Complaint Pack Framework;
- data and evidence cross boundaries only through controlled contracts;
- relevant subordinate documentation, security and regression requirements are satisfied.

## 18. Final statement

The Quaerens Platform is built around independent frameworks with clearly defined responsibilities.

The platform grows by extending frameworks rather than creating standalone products.

Every future decision begins with one question:

Which framework owns this responsibility?
